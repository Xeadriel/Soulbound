# Story and world

**Status:** Draft. These are the story notes so far; much is still brainstorming.

## The world: immortality without a death god

- Sentient beings (various races; not important for now, more can be added later) became **immortal** millennia ago by waging a war against the **god of death**. Immortal does not mean invincible.
- Side effect: people who die from **accidents, violence and other non-natural causes** become monsters, because their souls have nowhere to go. These monsters are the **Severed** (severed souls). Each one reflects the defining aspects of the person it was.
- Not much of this is understood. Maybe it was once, but that knowledge is long lost.
- The world struggles with **overpopulation** and constant **monster outbreaks**. Some groups want to bring the god of death back; others look for a solution to the severings.
- Early keyword notes: immortality, the death god is dead, an apocalypse from war, monsters born from deaths by unnatural causes and stress.

## The Hunter Order

- A loosely organized task force of independent people who clean up monsters, help with and prevent outbreaks, and clear infestations.
- They sometimes work in pairs or groups, also against other dangers: magical monsters, hostile organizations, hostile races, etc.
- The player characters are members of the order.

## The player characters

- **Twin sisters.** The second character is a **spirit**, cursed by the chalice, on a mission from the Hunter Order.
- **End goal:** getting the twin sister back to normal.
- The first dungeon's start room is the last fight without the ghost (second) character (see [design/dungeon-1.md](design/dungeon-1.md)).

## The blood chalice and the death god

- The **blood chalice** gives special abilities.
- The god (of death) is inside the chalice and wants to be revived (open idea). He manipulates the heroes into killing and feeding the chalice blood, so that monsters which otherwise can't be killed die. Giving blood makes him stronger for when he inevitably revives, but the heroes don't know this.
- He is too weak to grant death instantly. So everyone he tries to "finish off", the players included, has to fight his **minions**, which are a manifestation of his weakened power, in his **underworld**. This happens automatically and involuntarily whenever someone dies.
- Giving him someone's blood makes that underworld fight much harder.
- **Anyone who escapes the underworld is revived**, because causality has to make sure they are either dead or alive, and surviving the underworld is neither. Neither people nor the god know about this causality part; nobody is sure why, for example, a dismembered head reattaches.
- He tries to weaken the minions, since the player characters are useful to him, but the minions inevitably come with his existence.
- **The more bosses the players permanently kill, the more powerful the underworld becomes**, and so does the last boss fight if they don't side with the god at the end.
- Gameplay use: dying sends the players to the underworld, and fighting their way out revives them. The tutorial should show this by having the players die (see [design/items.md](design/items.md#chalice-of-death)).

## Ending ideas

**Status:** Idea.

- See the chalice choices in [design/items.md](design/items.md#chalice-of-death): killing bosses with the chalice leads toward a "bad ending" that revives the death god; entrapping them leads toward a good ending.
- **Ending plot twist (William's idea):** the death god knew he would be defeated and split himself and his power into fragments: one in the chalice and two living, moving through the world (the player characters). The players have to choose to become **Whole** or **Hole**.
